# Your Story Bank — A Worksheet for All 13 Story Types

The examples in `02_star_method_blueprints.md` and `03_L5_leadership_deep_dive.md` are illustrations. Interviewers probe for specifics, so **every story you tell must be yours**, true, and rehearsed out loud. This worksheet turns your experience into a bank of 10+ stories that covers every common prompt.

**Readiness targets (from the Google prep plan):**
- 10 or more stories written out and rehearsed aloud, each under 3 minutes.
- Any common behavioral question mapped to a story within 10 seconds.
- Every story survives 5 minutes of follow-up questions.

---

## Step 1 · Mine Your Raw Material (30 minutes)

List, without filtering, from the last 3-5 years:

```text
Projects I led or drove:
Biggest technical decisions I made or influenced:
Times I disagreed (with manager, peer, other team, senior engineer):
Things that went wrong on my watch (outages, missed deadlines, bad calls):
People I mentored or helped grow:
Hardest feedback I received:
Times I pushed back (on scope, quality, launch, ethics, user harm):
Processes or tools I improved:
Production incidents I handled:
Deadlines where I had to cut scope:
Conflicts or difficult working relationships:
Times I made sure someone's perspective was heard:
Ambiguous asks with no clear requirements:
```

Pick the richest 10-12. A good story has **real stakes, a decision you made, other people involved, and a measurable result.** Prefer recent and larger-scope stories for L5.

```text
TITLE (5 words):
PROMPTS IT ANSWERS (from the coverage matrix):
SITUATION (2-3 sentences: team, scale, problem, why it mattered):
TASK (my responsibility; what was ambiguous or hard):
ACTIONS (3-5 bullets, first person, each with a DECISION and WHY):
  - 
  - 
  - 
ALTERNATIVES I REJECTED AND WHY:
PEOPLE I HAD TO INFLUENCE (who, what they wanted, how I brought them along):
RESULT (numbers: latency / cost / revenue / incidents / adoption / time; people outcomes):
LESSON (what I learned; where I applied it since):
LIKELY FOLLOW-UPS + MY ANSWERS:
  - What was your specific role vs the team's?
  - What would you do differently?
  - What did your manager / the other person think?
  - How did you measure the result?
  - What was the hardest part?
```

## Step 3 · The 13 Story Types

For each: what the interviewer is probing, typical phrasings, what an L5-level answer shows, and traps.

### 1. Leading an ambiguous project end to end
- **Prompts:** "Tell me about a project you led." "A time you had to deliver with unclear requirements."
- **Probing:** comfort with ambiguity, ownership, leadership.
- **L5 signal:** you *defined* the problem (talked to users/stakeholders, found the real need), wrote the plan or design doc, aligned multiple parties, split work into milestones, delivered, and measured impact.
- **Trap:** a well-specified project where someone else defined the scope.

### 2. A technical decision with significant trade-offs
- **Prompts:** "Hardest technical decision?" "A time you chose between two approaches."
- **Probing:** RRK depth, judgment, how you decide.
- **L5 signal:** named alternatives, the criteria, data you gathered (prototype, benchmark), the trade-off you accepted, how you persuaded others, and what you'd revisit now.
- **Trap:** "we used Kafka because it's the best" — no alternatives, no cost.

### 3. Influencing without authority
- **Prompts:** "A time you convinced another team to change direction." "Getting buy-in from someone senior."
- **Probing:** leadership across boundaries.
- **L5 signal:** you understood their incentives and constraints, reframed the proposal in their terms, used data or a prototype, found a compromise or a shared goal, and they changed course.
- **Trap:** escalating to your manager as the main tactic.

### 4. Disagreeing with your manager or a peer
- **Prompts:** "Tell me about a conflict with your manager." "A time you disagreed with a decision."
- **Probing:** respect, courage, "disagree and commit."
- **L5 signal:** you raised it privately and early with evidence, listened, and either changed their mind or committed fully to the decision (and said so), with a clear outcome.
- **Trap:** a story where you were simply right and they were wrong, or where you complied silently.

### 5. A failure or mistake you owned
- **Prompts:** "Your biggest failure." "A mistake and what you learned."
- **Probing:** humility, ownership, learning.
- **L5 signal:** a **real** failure with consequences, your part stated plainly, how you mitigated, how you fixed the *system* (process, tests, alerts) so it can't recur, and evidence you changed.
- **Trap:** a disguised strength ("I worked too hard"), or blaming others.

### 6. Mentoring someone to greater ownership
- **Prompts:** "A time you helped a teammate grow." "How do you mentor?"
- **Probing:** force multiplication, caring about the team.
- **L5 signal:** what they could do before vs after, your specific actions (delegated real ownership, pairing, feedback cadence, sponsoring visibility), and their outcome (promotion, leading a project).
- **Trap:** "I answered their questions."

### 7. Receiving hard feedback and acting on it
- **Prompts:** "Toughest feedback you've received?" "A time feedback changed how you work."
- **Probing:** openness, self-awareness.
- **L5 signal:** the actual feedback (not softened), your honest first reaction, what you changed, and how you verified the change worked (e.g., asked again later).
- **Trap:** trivial feedback, or feedback you "proved wrong."

### 8. Pushing back for users or quality
- **Prompts:** "A time you stopped a launch." "Raised an uncomfortable concern."
- **Probing:** doing the right thing, user focus, courage.
- **L5 signal:** you identified the risk (privacy, data loss, accessibility, reliability), quantified it, proposed options (delay, reduced scope, launch behind a flag), and helped decide; the user impact was prevented.
- **Trap:** pushing back for your own preference with no user stake.

### 9. Resolving team conflict or working with a difficult colleague
- **Prompts:** "A difficult coworker." "Two teammates in conflict."
- **Probing:** empathy, collaboration.
- **L5 signal:** you sought to understand their perspective and pressures first, had a direct private conversation, found common goals, and the working relationship or the outcome improved.
- **Trap:** describing the colleague as the villain.

### 10. Prioritizing under a tight deadline
- **Prompts:** "A time you couldn't do everything." "Missed or nearly missed deadline."
- **Probing:** judgment, communication.
- **L5 signal:** how you decided what to cut (user impact, risk, reversibility), who you consulted, how you communicated early, and the result (shipped the core, cut items delivered later).
- **Trap:** "we worked weekends" as the solution.

### 11. Handling a production incident
- **Prompts:** "A time something broke in production." "Worked under pressure."
- **Probing:** calm, communication, ownership, systems thinking.
- **L5 signal:** mitigated first (rollback, failover), communicated status on a cadence, coordinated people, found root cause, led a blameless postmortem, and drove prevention (tests, alerts, canaries) with measurable improvement.
- **Trap:** a hero debugging story with no prevention.

### 12. Improving a process or the team's productivity
- **Prompts:** "Something you improved that wasn't asked of you." "Challenged the status quo."
- **Probing:** bias to action, challenging the status quo.
- **L5 signal:** noticed a recurring cost, measured it, proposed and piloted a change, got adoption beyond yourself, and measured before/after (hours saved, incident reduction, faster reviews).
- **Trap:** a tool nobody else adopted.

### 13. Making sure quieter or different perspectives were heard
- **Prompts:** "How do you make meetings inclusive?" "A time you included someone who wasn't being heard."
- **Probing:** inclusion, caring about the team.
- **L5 signal:** a concrete moment (invited input, changed a meeting format, credited someone's idea, adjusted for time zones or language), and what that perspective changed in the outcome.
- **Trap:** a generic statement of values with no specific example.

## Step 5 · Rehearse

1. **Say each story out loud**, timed. Target 2-3 minutes. Cut Situation first if too long.
2. **Record** one full run; listen for "we" where you mean "I", missing numbers, and filler.
3. **Follow-up drill:** have a friend (or write for yourself) five follow-ups per story; answer each in under 60 seconds.
4. **10-second mapping drill:** shuffle the prompts above; name the story within 10 seconds.
5. **Mock behavioral round** with someone who will interrupt and probe.

## Quality Checklist Per Story

- [ ] It's true, specific, and mine.
- [ ] The situation is 2-3 sentences, the action is the majority.
- [ ] I say "I" for my actions and credit others for theirs.
- [ ] At least one decision with alternatives and reasoning.
- [ ] Numbers in the result.
- [ ] The scope is appropriate for L5 (ambiguity, cross-team, or multiplier effect).
- [ ] A lesson, and evidence I applied it.
- [ ] I can answer "what would you do differently?" honestly.
