# Leadership Without Authority & Mentorship Deep Dive

The two most critical signals for a Senior (L5) Behavioral interview at Google are Leadership Without Authority and Mentorship.

## 1. Leadership Without Authority

At L4, you are expected to solve problems within your team. At L5, you are expected to solve problems that cross team boundaries, often where you have no formal authority.

### Core Signals
- **Empathy and Perspective-Taking:** Understanding the goals, incentives, and constraints of other teams.
- **Influence and Persuasion:** Framing technical proposals in terms that align with the other party's goals.
- **Compromise and Negotiation:** Finding a win-win, rather than insisting on technical purity.

### Traps
- **Escalating too quickly:** Involving managers should be a last resort.
- **Technical arrogance:** Assuming your solution is objectively best without understanding the other team's constraints.
- **Ignoring incentives:** Telling a team to do work that hurts their metrics.

### Follow-up Questions
- "What would you have done if they still said no?"
- "How did you know what their goals were?"
- "Did you have to give up anything to get their agreement?"

## 2. Mentorship and Growing Others

L5 engineers are force multipliers. They don't just write code; they increase the capacity of the team around them.

### Core Signals
- **Sponsorship:** Delegating real ownership, not just tasks.
- **Coaching:** Answering questions with questions to build problem-solving capability.
- **Feedback:** Providing actionable, specific, and timely feedback.

### Traps
- **Confusing mentoring with helping:** "I helped them debug" is L4. "I taught them how to debug" is L5.
- **Taking over:** Stepping in and finishing the task when they struggle.
- **Generic feedback:** "Good job" or "Needs improvement" without specific examples.

### Follow-up Questions
- "How did you measure their growth?"
- "What was the hardest part of letting them own the project?"
- "Have they since mentored anyone else?"
